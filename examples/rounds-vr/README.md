# ROUNDS VR

A 3D, VR version of the duel shooter ROUNDS, on `@kithin/engine` (a clone of `examples/standalone-game`). **The maps, the cards and the physics are the
game's own**, read from its files (see `docs/rounds-vr.md`): a side view with a little depth, like the 2D game ported to 3D, on 24 of its maps. First to 2 points wins
a round; after every round the one who lost **shoots a card out of three** and gets stronger; first to 5 (or 3) rounds wins the match.
You play blue; the orange side is a rival bot, or a second player.

```sh
npm run dev -w rounds-vr          # http://localhost:5175: builds the world first, and rebuilds and reloads when src/world or src/script change
npm run check -w rounds-vr        # type-checks the scripts, lints the world (panels, signs, reach)
node --experimental-transform-types --test tests/roundsVr.test.mjs
```

Kithin's address, app id and sign-in work as in `standalone-game` (see its README): they are only needed for an account.

## Playing

1. You arrive in the **menu**, a room of its own with a panel: who is in the room, **INVITE A PLAYER**, the *rival level* (easy, normal, hard),
   and the *match* (first to 3 or 5 rounds). Nobody picks a mode: **the room decides**. Alone, you play the rival (a bot). **INVITE A PLAYER**
   opens the game's own menu on the invitation: choose who can join, the session is hosted and the room code is there. When someone joins,
   they are your opponent, with no other choice to make. Press **START**: the gun goes to your right hand and the shield to your left (the
   other player gets the orange ones), and a countdown begins. The rival does nothing until it ends.
2. **Trigger of the gun** shoots (hold to keep firing): 3 bullets, 0.3 s apart, then a 2 s reload. **Trigger of the shield** raises it for 0.3 s
   (nothing hurts); it takes 4 s to come back. The gun's radial menu also has *Block*, *Reload* and *Quit match* (the way on a desktop, which has one hand).
3. **A button** (right controller; Space on a desktop) jumps (4 units: 2.5 m). **Jump again against a wall to climb it.** Bullets fall: aim above
   what is far away, as in the game. Two hits (55 each) kill (100 HP).
4. A small **HUD** follows your head, above the line of sight: both healths, the points, the round, the magazine and the shield. The rival's
   health is a bar over it. A hit flashes red, holds the one hit for a moment and shows the damage; a kill slows the world.
5. A point ends a duel; both fighters are put back on their pads. After a round the loser's three cards hang in front of them: **shoot** one.
   At the end the gear is taken off and you are back in the menu. The gear is given by START: there is nothing to pick up.

## How it is made

The game is **one script**, `src/script/director.ts` (TypeScript, with `math.ts`, `stats.ts` and `types.ts` beside it), plus a small script on each
gun and shield (`brain.ts`). They are bundled into code blocks when the world is built (`src/world/script.ts`) and checked like any other code.
`src/world/*.ts` builds the slots, `fragments.ts` writes each map as a scene fragment (`static/content/maps/*.kfrag`) and `build.ts` writes the world
package. The engine does the rest through generic parts made for this game (see `docs/rounds-vr.md`): the `characterController` for the player's
body, `ctx.physics` for what a bullet meets, fragments for the maps, `ctx.time`, `ctx.audio.playAt`, haptics and pushes, and
`@kithin/engine/testing` for the tests.

## Look and lighting

The four looks (`src/world/style.ts`) take turns over the maps: **Ruins**, **Desert**, **Cyber** and **Ice**, Poly Haven (CC0) materials loaded from the
library's CDN, with a sky for each. The lobby is an arcade parlour whose cabinet is the menu. The lighting is **baked**: `npm run bake -w rounds-vr` writes a
lighting volume for the lobby and one for each map (`static/content/lighting-*.lvol`, found by name in `src/world/lighting.ts`, so no hash is written by hand);
bake again after changing the geometry or the lights: only the regions whose scene changed are baked (`--force` bakes all, `map-3 lobby` names some,
`--threads=N` limits the cores), and a picture of each is left in `.bake/`. Then `npm run world`.
Models and music are generated by `tools/make-models.py` and `tools/make-audio.py`; the maps and the cards by `tools/extract-maps.py` and
`tools/extract-cards.py` from a copy of ROUNDS (`pip install UnityPy TypeTreeGeneratorAPI`). The results are committed; run them only to change something.
`npm run dev` watches `src/world`, `src/script` and `static/content` (`?nopause` skips the pause screen).

## Tuning

`src/world/data.ts` has the numbers, each with where in the game it was read (`SCALE`, `TUNING`, `MOVEMENT`, `HUMAN`). The cards are `cards.json` converted
by `convert` (the game's own multipliers and additions); `PLAYABLE` lists the ones that can be played here. The script imports them, so the tests
follow what you change.
